# Temple Builder: concept (1 Corinthians 3)

*"For no one can lay any foundation other than the one already laid, which is Jesus Christ. If anyone builds on this foundation using gold, silver, costly stones, wood, hay or straw, their work will be shown for what it is… The fire will test the quality of each person's work."* 1 Corinthians 3:11–13

**Prototype:** `docs/temple-prototype.html`. It simulates days so you can watch weeks and months of growth and run a fire test. It is not connected to the app yet.

This concept draws on two research passes:
- **Theology and child development:** Fee's commentary on 1 Corinthians, Revelation 21, Isaiah 54, Exodus 28, and studies of honesty in children.
- **Visual and game design:** Townscaper, Monument Valley, Minecraft, Finch, Forest and Habitica.

## The big idea

Each child builds their own **temple** on a foundation that is already there: Jesus, the cornerstone. It glows, carries a cross, and never burns.

- **Every block is one real choice the child logged**, so they can tap any block and see what built it.
- **Good choices** add gold, silver, gems and pearl.
- **Wood, hay and stubble** fill the gaps from fights, tantrums and rude words.
- **Once a week the fire comes.** Wood, hay and stubble burn away and leave glowing empty settings. Gold and gems come out *brighter* (refined).
- **Precious stones fill the burnt gaps first**, and each filled gap heals with a **gold seam**. Like kintsugi, grace makes the building more beautiful where it was broken.

## What builds what

| Logged | Material | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A prayer, including Fruit Tree prayers and breath prayers | **Gold** | Prayers are golden bowls of incense (Rev 5:8). Gold survives fire. |
| Kind words, making peace | **Silver** | "The tongue of the righteous is choice silver" (Prov 10:20). It is the opposite of rude words. |
| Love | Carnelian | The red stone around God's throne (Rev 4:3) |
| Joy | Topaz | The golden "sunshine" stone |
| Peace | Sapphire | "Covenant of peace… foundations with sapphires" (Isa 54:10–11) |
| Patience | Chalcedony | Forms slowly, in layers |
| Kindness | Emerald | The emerald rainbow of God's covenant kindness (Rev 4:3, Gen 9) |
| Goodness | Chrysolite | The "golden stone"; good works shine (Matt 5:16) |
| Faithfulness | Jasper | The first foundation stone and the city wall (Rev 21:18–19) |
| Gentleness | Beryl | Calm, sea-blue |
| Self-control | Amethyst | Greek *amethystos*, "not drunk": an ancient symbol of self-mastery |
| Owned up and made it right | **Pearl** | The gates of pearl (Rev 21:21); a pearl forms around a hurt |
| Fighting a sibling | Wood | Burns |
| Tantrum or disobedience | Hay | Burns |
| Rude words | Stubble | Burns |

The gem-to-fruit links are symbolic choices. The app should say so: *"A picture of 1 Corinthians 3, not a scorecard. God's love for you never changes."*

**No double logging.** Prayers and fruit already logged on the Fruit Tree flow straight into the temple as gold and gems. The tree stays the grace-only space and never records failures. The temple is where the honest end-of-day review happens.

## End-of-day logging (2 minutes at bedtime)

- **Eliana (12)** logs her own day; a parent glances over it.
- **Annabelle (8)** logs during a short bedtime chat with a parent.
- **Judah (6)** logs with a parent, using picture cards and read-aloud.
- **Honesty always wins.** If logging hay only ever hurts, kids learn to hide it; the research is clear on this (Talwar & Lee 2011; Lee et al. 2014). So:
  - Hay the child **owns up to** goes on the building, but that night they can **make it right** (say sorry, fix it), and that adds a **pearl**.
  - Hay that a parent reports is never treated as better than hay the child confessed.
  - Never add extra penalty for something confessed.
- **Kids log only their own part** ("I…", never "she…"). When siblings make up, both earn silver (Matt 5:9).
- **Parents never use it as a threat** ("that's going on your tower!").

## The fire: weekly, as a family ritual

**Weekly is recommended over daily.** Paul's fire is one future Day, and a daily fire leaves no time to make things right. A **Saturday-evening "Sabbath fire"** keeps it away from Sunday's ministry load.

**The 10-minute ritual:**
1. Dim the lights and light a real candle.
2. Read 1 Corinthians 3:11 aloud.
3. Give a last chance to own up and make things right. Pearls can still be added.
4. Run each child's fire, **youngest first**. The child watches standing beside Jesus; they are never in the fire.
5. Celebrate what shone: "Look how bright your gold is!"
6. Parents show their own temple and name one of their own hay blocks, so the kids see that grown-ups are still growing too.
7. Speak the blessing from Numbers 6:24–26 over each child. Close with Romans 8:1: "no condemnation".

**The fire, beat by beat (about 12 seconds, never scary):**
1. The sky dims to dusk and the caption reads "The fire will test the quality of each one's work."
2. Flames sweep across the building.
3. Wood, hay and stubble flash, char and crumble into rising sparks.
4. Gold and gems glow white-hot: "Refined!"
5. Glowing empty settings show where something burnt.
6. Dawn returns and a dove appears: "What you built with God remains."

## Stability: your call (the prototype has both)

After the fire, the app checks the gaps in the part of the building that isn't finished yet:

| Gaps | What happens |
|---|---|
| Under 10% | Strong and steady, just a few glowing empty settings |
| 10–25% | Hairline cracks and a gentle wobble |
| 25–40% | The building leans and scaffolding goes up: "builders are coming" |
| Over 40% | **Topple** or **Gentle** (below) |

- **Topple** (your original idea): the weakest row of the unfinished section and everything above it falls. Gold and gems are **saved and laid again**; only wood and hay are lost. The caption reads *"He himself will be saved, but only as through fire" (1 Cor 3:15). Jesus, the foundation, never falls.*
- **Gentle** (what the theology research recommends, especially for Judah): nothing falls. The temple can't grow higher until the gaps are filled. The research points out that Paul's fire burns materials but never brings down the structure, and the builder is always saved.

Two rules apply either way, so it never feels hopeless:
- **The foundation and finished stages are sealed and can never fall.** A stage seals once it is complete with only fire-proof blocks.
- **Losses stay small.** Precious blocks are never lost, and a weekly fire keeps each week's damage manageable.

## How it grows over weeks and months

At about 5 blocks a day, one temple takes about 3 months:

| Stage | Blocks | About when |
|---|---|---|
| 🧱 Foundation | 80 | weeks 1–2 |
| 🏛️ Walls and windows | 106 | weeks 3–6 |
| 🗼 Four towers and battlements | 74 | weeks 7–9 |
| 🚪 Pearl gate | 16 | week 10 |
| 🕌 Golden dome | 82 | weeks 11–13 |

**Then the city (months 4–12, the New Jerusalem of Revelation 21):**
- Each finished temple joins a shared **city map**, and the three children's temples meet on a **street of gold**.
- Each month adds one of the **12 foundation layers** of the city wall, one gem at a time, so a year completes the city.
- Pearls build the **12 gates**. These are a shared family goal, never a competition.
- Each child's name is engraved on a stone "carried on the heart" of the High Priest (Exodus 28:29). Jesus is our High Priest (Hebrews 4:14–16).

**Keeping it fresh at month 3 (version 2 ideas):**
- A ghost blueprint shows where the next blocks go.
- Lamps glow at night, and the scene follows the seasons and church calendar (Advent lights, Easter sunrise, Pentecost banners).
- Children name their towers ("Patience Tower").
- Visitors appear: doves, a lamb, travellers walking in through the gates.
- A **timelapse** replays months of building in 10 seconds.
- A weekly photo album keeps a before-and-after card for each fire.

## Guardrails

1. **Not works-righteousness.** The foundation is there first. Gems are called "fruit of the Spirit", grown by God, and the weekly talk is about the heart, not the score.
2. **No hiding.** Owning up is always the best outcome, and making it right earns a pearl.
3. **No fear or shame.** The child is never in the fire. Use scaffolding, not ruins. Gentle mode is available, and Judah has a lower cap on hay.
4. **No comparison.** One temple shows at a time, there are no rankings or height numbers, and the gates are a shared family goal.
5. **Not overreaching.** It is "a picture, not a scorecard", and 1 Corinthians 4:5 and Romans 8:1 are read at the ritual.

## Update: the brick tower (prototype v4)

Joel chose concept-art #4, the leaning tower on a sea cliff, then asked for missing pieces that make sense like Jenga, more pull towards gold and more push away from hay. This version is based on research into how games handle structural support (Jenga, Valheim, Minecraft, Fortnite, 7 Days to Die, bridge games) and into motivation (Minecraft light, SimCity blight, Stardew quality, Duolingo streak repair, and studies of the overjustification and IKEA effects).

**The two-hands rule.** The tower is laid like real brickwork, so every brick rests on the two bricks below it.
- **Both hands there:** the brick is solid.
- **One hand burnt away:** the brick cracks and wobbles.
- **No hands left:** the brick falls, and the bricks above it check again, so collapses chain upward row by row.
- **Hay low in the tower is the most dangerous**, because many bricks end up resting on it.

**No surprises.** All week, bricks that would fall at the next fire glow red. A banner and the forecast panel give the count, for example "21 good bricks will fall at the next fire!" Nothing is random.

**Gold is strong:**
- A brick resting on one gold hand doesn't crack, because gold spans the gap.
- A full row of gold and silver acts as a beam, a new ground that stops chains.
- A level that is complete and has no hay is sealed and can never fall.

**Light vs rot:**
- Gold gives out light to itself and its neighbours.
- Hay rots day by day: fresh, damp, mouldy, then swarming with flies.
- Each night, flies spread to nearby gems and dull them, unless those gems are in gold's light.
- Doves land only on gold with no flies nearby.

**Make it right:** owning up and fixing it turns the newest rotten brick into redeemed stone and adds a pearl.

**Rubble, not wipe-out:**
- Fallen gold, gems and pearls land in a rubble pile, and each new good brick lifts one back into place.
- Silver and stone that fall are lost.

This keeps real stakes without destroying everything. The IKEA-effect research says kids stop caring once what they built is gone.
