# Temple Builder: three new angles (2026-10-15)

Joel was lukewarm on the tower, so we researched two things:
- other biblical pictures of testing;
- what keeps kids coming back to daily habit games (self-determination theory, the IKEA effect, Habitica and Finch post-mortems, Tamagotchi).

**What the research says the tower got wrong:**
- **No choices:** bricks appear on their own, so it's a simulation rather than a game.
- **No character to love.** Kids bond with characters, not buildings.
- **Destroying what kids built kills motivation.** In a field study, Habitica's punishment backfired for every participant, and kids stop valuing what they made once it's destroyed.

**What works:**
- one real choice every session;
- a satisfying reveal: crack, polish, pour;
- losses that can be undone;
- about 90 seconds per child;
- cooperative family goals.

---

## A. The Refiner's Forge (recommended)

*Malachi 3:3, "He will sit as a refiner and purifier of silver"; 1 Peter 1:7; Revelation 4:10.*

Each child is an apprentice in a glowing forge, with a little fox blacksmith as their companion. The fire refines rather than destroys, which is the closest match to 1 Corinthians 3.

**The 90-second daily session:**
1. The child's choices arrive as ore.
2. They tap to crack open rough gems and rub to polish them.
3. They pour gold into the crucible.
4. **The choice:** which piece to work on (a crown, a cup, a sceptre or a shield) and which setting each gem goes into.

**Bad choices:** these drop grey slag into the crucible.

**Sunday firing:**
- The family watches the slag hiss off.
- The karat number settles: a gold week climbs from 9k to 14k, 18k and 24k, while a heavy week drops karat they spent weeks building and the piece cools dull.
- Owning up during the week lets the child "skim the slag" themselves, so they lose less.
- Nothing is ever smashed.

**Over months:** finished pieces go into a trophy gallery. At the end of the year, the kids lay their crowns before the throne (Revelation 4:10).

---

## B. Nehemiah's Wall (family co-op)

*Nehemiah 2–6: each family rebuilt the section beside their own house, and the wall was finished in 52 days.*

**Each child's section:** the child rebuilds their own named, bannered section of Jerusalem's wall.

**The choice:** tap a stone, then tap where it goes (a gate, a tower or a weak spot).

**Bad choices:** these leave gaps. Sanballat's night raiders creep toward the gaps and darken that part of the city until it's repaired.

**Grace in action:** a sibling can spend their gold to help mend your breach.

**The city grows:** it fills with life behind the wall, with markets, lamps and townspeople.

**The season:** it lasts 52 days and ends in a dedication feast with a real family treat. Then a new city begins.

---

## C. Pilgrim's Road (journey)

*Hebrews 12:1, "throw off everything that hinders"; Pilgrim's Progress.*

**The journey:** each child's pilgrim and their lamb companion walk an illustrated map towards the Celestial City.

**The choices:** good choices move them forward and earn items. The child chooses paths at forks and what to pack.

**Bad choices:** these add stones to the backpack. At the weekly Hill Difficulty, a heavy pack slides them back.

**Grace:** stopping at the wayside cross to own up makes the whole burden roll away. That's the most powerful grace moment of the three.
