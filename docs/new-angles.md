# Temple Builder: three new angles (2026-10-15)

Joel was lukewarm on the tower, so we researched two things:
- other biblical pictures of testing;
- what keeps kids coming back to daily habit games (self-determination theory, the IKEA effect, Habitica and Finch post-mortems, Tamagotchi).

**What the research says the tower got wrong:**
- **No choices:** bricks appear on their own, so it's a simulation rather than a game.
- **No character to love.** Kids bond with characters, not buildings.
- **Destroying what kids built kills motivation.** In a field study, Habitica's punishment backfired for every participant, and kids stop valuing what they made once it's destroyed.

**What works:**
- one real choice every session;
- a satisfying reveal: crack, polish, pour;
- losses that can be undone;
- about 90 seconds per child;
- cooperative family goals.

---

## A. The Refiner's Forge (recommended)

*Malachi 3:3, "He will sit as a refiner and purifier of silver"; 1 Peter 1:7; Revelation 4:10.*

Each child is an apprentice in a glowing forge, with a little fox blacksmith as their companion. The fire refines rather than destroys, which is the closest match to 1 Corinthians 3.

**The 90-second daily session:**
1. The child's choices arrive as ore.
2. They tap to crack open rough gems and rub to polish them.
3. They pour gold into the crucible.
4. **The choice:** which piece to work on (a crown, a cup, a sceptre or a shield) and which setting each gem goes into.

**Bad choices:** these drop grey slag into the crucible.

**Sunday firing:**
- The family watches the slag hiss off.
- The karat number settles: a gold week climbs from 9k to 14k, 18k and 24k, while a heavy week drops karat they spent weeks building and the piece cools dull.
- Owning up during the week lets the child "skim the slag" themselves, so they lose less.
- Nothing is ever smashed.

**Over months:** finished pieces go into a trophy gallery. At the end of the year, the kids lay their crowns before the throne (Revelation 4:10).

## B. Nehemiah's Wall (family co-op)

*Nehemiah 2–6: each family rebuilt the section beside their own house, and the wall was finished in 52 days.*

**Each child's section:** the child rebuilds their own named, bannered section of Jerusalem's wall.

**The choice:** tap a stone, then tap where it goes (a gate, a tower or a weak spot).

**Bad choices:** these leave gaps. Sanballat's night raiders creep toward the gaps and darken that part of the city until it's repaired.

**Grace in action:** a sibling can spend their gold to help mend your breach.

**The city grows:** it fills with life behind the wall, with markets, lamps and townspeople.

**The season:** it lasts 52 days and ends in a dedication feast with a real family treat. Then a new city begins.

---

## C. Pilgrim's Road (journey)

*Hebrews 12:1, "throw off everything that hinders"; Pilgrim's Progress.*

**The journey:** each child's pilgrim and their lamb companion walk an illustrated map towards the Celestial City.

**The choices:** good choices move them forward and earn items. The child chooses paths at forks and what to pack.

**Bad choices:** these add stones to the backpack. At the weekly Hill Difficulty, a heavy pack slides them back.

**Grace:** stopping at the wayside cross to own up makes the whole burden roll away. That's the most powerful grace moment of the three.

**Extra:** Eliana could read an abridged Pilgrim's Progress alongside.

# Five gem concepts (Joel likes new precious stones appearing)

## 1. Crystal Dragon (companion)
**The idea:** a baby dragon hatches, and every scale is one of the child's gems.

**Each day:**
- Good choices give gems.
- The child **chooses** which gem to feed it, and that gem becomes a new scale.

**Bad choices:** sticky mud scales with flies, so the dragon is itchy and grumpy.

**Weekly test:** the dragon breathes fire on itself and the mud burns off. A muddy dragon is sleepy for a day, but it never dies, and making it right cleans a scale.

**Over months:** it grows from hatchling to young dragon to giant, and the colour patterns can be customised.

## 2. Stained-Glass Window (picture reveal)
**The idea:** each gem is a piece of coloured glass, and over weeks a Bible picture slowly appears (the Good Shepherd, then Noah's rainbow, then the empty tomb).

**Each day:** the child **chooses** where each piece goes.

**Bad choices:** soot and cobwebs on panes.

**Weekly test:** "sunrise". Light shines through and the clean glass glows on the floor, but sooty panes stay dark and must be scrubbed by making it right.

**Over months:** a finished window is hung in a cathedral gallery and a new picture starts.

## 3. The High Priest's Breastplate (Exodus 28)
**The idea:** 12 gem settings in gold, each stone engraved with a name, as Aaron carried the tribes' names "over his heart" (Exodus 28:29).

**Each day:** good choices give rough stones to **polish** (rub to shine) and set. The child chooses whose name to engrave: someone they prayed for.

**Bad choices:** mud dulls a stone.

**Weekly test:** the lamp is lit and clean stones blaze while muddy ones stay dark.

**Over months:** the breastplate, then the golden plate engraved "Holy to the Lord", then the full priestly garments.

**The deepest picture:** Jesus is our High Priest who carries our names (Hebrews 4:14).

## 4. Gem Mine (dig and discover)
**The idea:** a cutaway hill. Each good choice gives a pickaxe swing, and the child **chooses where to dig** to uncover gem veins and gold nuggets, with a mole companion.

**Bad choices:** rotten props and mouldy hay block the tunnels.

**Weekly test:** a cave-in check. Tunnels shored up with rotten props collapse and bury the gems beyond them until the tunnel is cleared.

**Over months:** a deeper and deeper mine with rarer gems and a growing treasure room.

## 5. Gem Lighthouse (Matthew 5:14, "You are the light of the world")
**The idea:** each gem becomes a panel in the lighthouse lens, and the beam gets brighter and more colourful.

**Bad choices:** cracked, grimy panels.

**Weekly test:** "storm night". The beam has to guide ships safely home, and a bright lens rescues more boats. Rescued sailors move into a growing harbour village, so good choices visibly help others.

# The Mine and the Temple (gem mine + 1 Corinthians 3)

Joel liked the gem mine (digging, choosing, pretty gems, collapse) but wanted a tighter link to 1 Corinthians 3.

**Below ground, the mine:**
- Each good choice is a pickaxe swing. The child taps where to dig and cracks open gold and gems.
- Each bad choice means propping a tunnel with wood, hay or stubble, Paul's own materials. These props are mouldy and buzzing with flies.

**Above ground, the temple:**
- Mined gems ride up in a minecart, and the child chooses where to set them.
- The temple sits on the foundation "Jesus Christ" (1 Cor 3:10–11, "each one should build with care").

**Weekly fire (1 Cor 3:13):**
- Fire sweeps down through the temple and the mine.
- Gold and gems come out refined and brighter.
- Every wood, hay or stubble prop burns and its tunnel caves in, burying the gems beyond it until they're dug out again.
- The temple the child built is never wrecked; the loss happens underground.

**Make it right:** owning up swaps a hay prop for stone before the fire.
