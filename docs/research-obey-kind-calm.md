# What works: obeying straight away, kind words, calming down (ages 6–12)

Research summary for the Morning Habits app, 24 Nov 2026 (web research by a helper agent; evidence strength noted).

**Short answer:** catch the good often and name it, give small rewards straight away, practise the skill when everyone is calm, keep penalties tiny. In an app, the parent's spoken praise does more than the coins.

## 1. Obeying straight away
- **Clear single instruction, then praise that names it** (PCIT, strong evidence). App: one-tap "Obeyed straight away" with a praise line for the parent to say ("You came the first time I asked, thank you").
- **Practise obeying as a game when calm** (Kazdin). App: a 2-minute practice round of silly instructions (already in Build on the Rock as "Practice for gold", v73).
- **Small reward now beats a big reward later.** A chart alone with no praise rarely works.
- **Calm, short, predictable consequence**, then a fresh start. No lecture.
- **About 4–5 praises for every correction** (rough guide, mostly from classrooms). App: a weekly figure for the parent only.

## 2. Kind words to siblings
- Teach what kind words sound like (sibling programme "More Fun with Sisters and Brothers").
- Notice and praise kindness between siblings ("caught being kind").
- Repair after conflict with a set script: "I'm sorry I… / Will you forgive me? / What can I do to make it right?" (evidence for any one script is thin; fits confession and forgiveness).
- A shared family goal reduces competing.

## 3. Calming down from big feelings
- Name the feeling, then guide (emotion coaching, Tuning in to Kids: encouraging trials).
- Slow breathing practised when calm (Stanford study of 342 young children: lower body stress; evidence on anger itself is limited). App: breath prayer (v77).
- **Reward recovery, not just no tantrums:** "Calmed down" worth more than a normal good moment.
- A calm-down plan made in advance (each child picks 3 steps).

## Avoid
- Big expected prizes for things a child already enjoys (Deci, Koestner and Ryan 1999: can reduce a child's own interest).
- Coins with no spoken praise; large or growing penalties; taking back earned rewards.
- Showing children each other's scores side by side.
- Lectures in the heat of the moment; behaviour-only focus (heart behind behaviour; Ephesians 6:4, Colossians 3:21).

## Weekly review (3–5 minutes per child)
1. Two or three specific wins, read out by the parent. 2. One growing chart, against their own past only. 3. One hard moment, retold with what to try next time (no new punishment). 4. Pick one focus for next week. 5. A short verse or prayer tied to it, then a small celebration.

## Showing growth
- A 4–6 week bar of weekly good moments (weekly is steadier than daily).
- For tantrums, show "times calmed down" going up, not "tantrums" going down.
- "Best week yet" milestones. Compare only with themselves.
